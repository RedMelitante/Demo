Concrete · 5 min read

Exposed aggregate or pavers: which suits your Melbourne driveway?

Your driveway takes more punishment than any other part of the yard: the weight of cars, hot summer sun and, in much of Melbourne, reactive clay that swells in winter and shrinks in summer. Both exposed aggregate and pavers can handle it when they're built properly. The right pick comes down to looks, budget and how much upkeep you want.

Exposed aggregate

Concrete is poured, then the top layer is washed off to reveal the stones underneath. The finish is textured and non-slip, and with a good sealer it hides tyre marks and leaf stains well. It's usually the more cost-effective option for large areas, and there's no weeding between joints.

Pavers

Concrete, clay or natural stone pavers give a more tailored look and suit period homes and formal front gardens. If a section ever cracks or a pipe needs to be dug up, individual pavers can be lifted and relaid instead of cutting out a whole slab.

What matters most on clay

  • A compacted crushed-rock base of the right depth for vehicles.
  • Steel reinforcement and well-placed control joints for concrete.
  • Falls and drainage that move water away from the house and the driveway edges.

Not sure which suits your place? We're happy to talk through both on site and quote either option.

Paving · 4 min read

Bluestone, travertine or porcelain: choosing a pool surround for Melbourne summers

A pool surround has to look good, stay cool enough to walk on barefoot, and grip when it's wet. Here's how the three most popular choices compare.

Travertine

Light-coloured and naturally textured, travertine stays among the coolest underfoot in full sun. Its soft, warm tones suit coastal and Mediterranean-style gardens. Tumbled or textured finishes give good grip around the water, and it should be sealed to keep it looking fresh.

Bluestone

A Melbourne classic that suits period and contemporary homes alike. It's dense and hard-wearing, but darker stone holds more heat, so choose a sawn or textured finish and think about shade around seating areas.

Porcelain

Very low maintenance and consistent in colour, with outdoor-rated tiles available in stone and timber looks. Choose an external, slip-rated finish for pool areas, and lighter colours to keep surface temperatures down.

Whatever you choose

  • Ask for a slip rating suited to wet, barefoot areas.
  • Plan falls so splash water drains away from the pool and the house.
  • Coping and paving should be laid on a solid base so edges don't move.

Drainage · 4 min read

Building on Melbourne clay: why drainage comes before paving

Large parts of Melbourne sit on reactive clay. It swells when it's wet and shrinks as it dries out, which is why you see cracked paths, sunken patios and garden beds that stay boggy all winter.

The good news: most of those problems are avoidable if the groundwork is done right before a single paver goes down.

What good groundwork looks like

  • Falls away from the house. Paving should shed water away from walls and footings, never towards them.
  • Ag drains and stormwater pits where water naturally collects, connected to a legal point of discharge.
  • A compacted crushed-rock base under paths, patios and driveways, so loads spread evenly.
  • Soil improvement in garden beds with gypsum and organic matter to help clay drain and plants root.

Signs your yard needs attention

Puddles that sit for days after rain, damp walls near paving, lawn that turns to mud, or cracks that open up every summer. Sorting drainage first costs less than replacing paving later.

FAQ

Questions we often get.

How much does landscaping cost in Melbourne?+

It depends on the size of the space, access, materials and how much hard landscaping is involved. A planting refresh costs far less than a full build with paving and retaining walls. After a site visit we'll give you an itemised quote so you can see exactly where the money goes.

Can you build from my landscape architect's plans?+

Yes. We're happy to work from existing design plans, or offer guidance and help shape a design if you're starting from scratch.

How long does a typical backyard project take?+

Smaller jobs like planting or new turf can take a few days. Full builds with paving, walls and drainage usually run a few weeks. Melbourne weather can shift things a little, and we'll keep you posted. You'll get a timeframe with your quote.

Do I need a permit for a retaining wall?+

Taller walls, or walls near a boundary or other structures, can need a building permit and engineering in Victoria. We'll flag it during the site visit and point you in the right direction with your council before any work starts.

What's the best time of year to landscape in Melbourne?+

Hard landscaping can happen most of the year. For planting, autumn and spring are ideal because the soil is warm and there's rain about. Summer planting is fine with good irrigation; we just plan for it.

Can you make my garden low-maintenance and water-wise?+

Definitely. Hardy natives, good mulch, drip irrigation on a timer and well-planned paving areas can cut weekend gardening right down while keeping the garden green through summer.

Which suburbs do you cover?+

We're based in Malvern East and mainly work across Melbourne's south-east and inner east, from Toorak and Brighton out to Glen Waverley and Box Hill. Call us if you're further afield.
